Sharpest finding per layer

The provenance claim is the auditor kill

“Immutable evidence fabric” resting on a key-presence lint is the claim most likely to fail a probe.

Fix · soften to “declares a trust anchor,” or wire real verification into the walker.

HR is a silent single point of failure

A wrong-but-well-formed HR value silently re-scopes access; the drift engine only catches codebook-invalid values.

Fix · an HR-ingest quarantine gate (staleness · null-placement · improbable-delta).

No path into canon — and canon cheats to reach it

The 66-book corpus lives in inbox/ (which canon must not cite), yet an adapter hard-codes an inbox/ path and emits nothing.

Fix · decide AAN’s destiny — promote to canon, or accept it as external and drop the reference.

Two risks that hit all three

Positioning: “universal” in doctrine, “federal” in reality

ADR-085 wants a vertical-agnostic core, but the orchestrator carries 86 federal references, every active adapter is gcc-moderate, OrgPath’s marquee instance is OPM HRIT, and AAN is the federal program. The universal story isn’t yet in the module layout or a second vertical.

Bus-factor

139 ADRs + ~70 canon docs + the OrgPath codebook chain + a 66-book corpus — and 63 of 64 commits are one author. The conceptual surface is likely navigable only by its author: a continuity and adoption risk pre-1.0.

Fix first — cheapest, highest-credibility

Language-tighten the three over-claims to match what’s built — UIAO provenance, OrgPath AWS/GCP/VMware “first-class,” AAN “generated” crosswalk. Mostly one-file edits.

Reconcile the three decision-vs-doc lags — rewrite VISION to ADR-085; land the ADR-127 doc sweep; regenerate the AAN figures. Add a CI lint each so they can’t drift again.

Ship one proof-of-life per axis — a non-federal vertical skeleton (UIAO), one cloud transport e.g. AWS Identity Center (OrgPath), the crosswalk generator (AAN).

Address the two structural risks deliberately — the HR-ingest quarantine gate (OrgPath) and a documented AAN → canon promotion decision.

None of this is a rearchitecture. All three are strong enough underneath that their main enemy is their own presentation getting ahead of their code — the same lesson the AAN critiques already landed, now one level up across the whole stack.
