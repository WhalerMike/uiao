**Closure-Necessity — alternate-path rebuttals (†).** For each necessity anchor, the strongest alternative a reviewer might propose and the specific reason it fails to close the control:

- **AC-2** — Strongest alternative: "directory-hygiene sweeps and access reviews keep accounts aligned." Fails AC-2 because alignment with termination/transfer presumes a machine-readable personnel truth to align to; employment facts originate in the HR system of record — no scanner or review cadence generates the fact of employment, it can only copy it, late.
- **IA-4** — Strongest alternative: "the directory prevents identifier reuse." Fails IA-4 because non-reuse and rehire correlation require durable memory of persons, not accounts; directories track accounts and forget them at deletion. Only the HR system of record, whose subject is the person, survives the end of every account.
- **PS-4** — Strongest alternative: "email/checklist offboarding disables access." Fails PS-4 because the clock starts at an event only the HR system records as a first-class fact — termination is not network-observable, and absence of logins is indistinguishable from leave; only an event-driven feed from the owner of the fact starts the automation on time.
- **PS-5** — Strongest alternative: "periodic access reviews catch transfers." Fails PS-5 because the formal transfer action is an HR record; to the network and directory the transferred person is the same account on the same device — no mechanism that cannot see the transfer can start the required review on time.
- **SI-12** — Strongest alternative: "reconstruct lineage from downstream copies at audit time." Fails SI-12 because lineage cannot be reconstructed afterward from copies — provenance must be emitted where the data originates and flows; designating and instrumenting one source is the only mechanism that produces lineage rather than asserting it.
